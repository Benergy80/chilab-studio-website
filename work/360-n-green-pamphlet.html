Reception desk for Sterling Bay at 360 N Green.

The work

A reception desk for Sterling Bay at 360 N Green, meant to read as smooth, aged, and modern at once. The whole underside of the desk curves inward, so it seems to float: you almost feel the pebble could roll away, and it doesn't. It sits balanced, and tells you the building is intentional and here for a reason.

The material question

A form that depends on looking effortless. Any flaw in the surface, any seam or dent, would break the sense of a single smooth stone.

The making

Industrial design, architecture, and interior detailing carried through one fabricated element. End pieces made by another shop came in with pour dents, and we concealed them until they matched the consistency and smoothness of everything else. The patina was applied on site, a finish developed for this desk.

In place

Applying a finish we had made up, in place, with no way to take it back to the shop, took a lot of confidence from the whole team. It is one of the details we are proudest of.
