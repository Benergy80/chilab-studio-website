Industry of the Ordinary, Driehaus Museum

Palimpsest

A Driehaus Museum doorknob, 3D scanned and recast in clay taken from the Chicago River.

The work

After the Great Chicago Fire of 1871, new buildings had to be brick or stone, and builders dug clay from the bed of the Chicago River to make the bricks that rebuilt the Nickerson Mansion, now the Driehaus Museum. Industry of the Ordinary took a brass doorknob from one of the doors that separates the family rooms from the servants' quarters and remade it in Chicago River clay, laid on a prone facsimile of its door: a trapdoor between upstairs and downstairs, and a work about the labor that grand houses make invisible.

The material question

An exact copy of a museum object, in a material that fights precision. The original could not leave its door, and river clay shrinks and moves as it dries and fires, so the duplicate had to come from data, then survive the kiln still reading as that particular knob.

The making

We 3D scanned the knob and its plate in place at the museum, cleaned the scan into a model, and split it into two molds, one for the knob and one for the backplate. Test pieces were pressed in the river clay and fired at different temperatures to dial in shrinkage and color before the final piece was made.

In place

Shown in Materialities at the Driehaus Museum from February 7 to April 27, 2025. The artists thanked Matthew Groves, H Schenck, Ally Reza and Ben Stagl. It was our second project with Industry of the Ordinary, after the cast glass Apple.
