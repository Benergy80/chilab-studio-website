Cast Aluminum Tessellation Wall
Architectural

A cast aluminum relief wall built from a repeating tile, developed for Gary Lee Partners.

The work

A relief wall running the length of the counter in a Gary Lee Partners interior. The design the client had committed to was bronze in the renderings, a rippling surface that should never visibly repeat.

The material question

The price had been promised before the wall was solved. Bronze at that scale, with that many unique pieces, was not possible for it. The question became how to keep the sense of a non-repeating ripple using a limited amount of tooling and a lighter metal.

The making

The answer was tessellation and edge symmetry: one repeating cast aluminum tile that reads as a continuous ripple rather than a grid. It was also the first time we turned aluminum into an oil rubbed bronze finish, and the hand rubbed patina added to the illusion in the best way. It bridged Ben Stagl's early training in patina at art foundries with his new work in Chicago as a designer and bespoke fabricator.

In place

The patina trick became a studio method. We used it again, years later, on the parametric cast feature wall at Uber Freight's headquarters.
