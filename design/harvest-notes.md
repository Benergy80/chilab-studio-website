## a murmuration (ohare-terminal-5)
- "A three-dimensional data visualization mapping over two hundred years of immigration to the region now called Illinois."
- Concept: "Demographic change as a constant within a democracy."
- ~600 discs, each an immigrant population by decade, scaled to Census data, colour-coded by continent of origin. Moving along the work, the eye traces how origins shifted across two centuries.
- 80 discs engraved, contributed by 80 community partners: words of home, welcome, community, each in a language native to its place of origin. English and Spanish translations online.
- Form: "Where cartography meets flocking." Armatures adapted from maps of the region's waterways, combining an early map narrated by an Indigenous mapmaker with a contemporary one.
- Arrangement generated with an adaptation of the BOIDS flocking algorithm, the murmuration of a starling flock. THIS IS WHY IT IS CALLED THAT.
- ChiLab role: precision production of ~600 discs; engraving of the 80 community discs; the mapped armature system engineered for a permanent installation.
- Credits: production management Devon Bonstein; project management Carrie Mackin; research Louie Escobar.
- CONFLICT: deck says 330 ft, site says 450 ft.

## St. Nicholas (st-nicholas-crosses + st-nicholas-icon-stands)
- Calatrava "marries Byzantine tradition with contemporary innovation, a symbol of unity and renewal after the attacks of September 11, reopened in 2022."
- Commission came through a referral from a mentor in the glass industry; approached by Koutsomitis Architects; tight timeline. Scope grew to include the metalwork and the bronze-and-marble pedestals.
- Casting: optically clear leaded glass prized for clarity; custom molds; a method minimising bubbles and flaws; "cooled with patience for clarity and longevity."
- HINGE: "Transparency lets light pass through — a metaphor for spiritual illumination."
- Icon stands: two pairs. Material harmony echoing the building; "ergonomic in worship, proportioned for the rhythms of the liturgy"; concealed integrated lighting for each icon.
- Ally Reza: "I feel like there is a special energy that is put into the pieces that we create because we really love and care about making things that last lifetimes."
- Method: 3D modeling, CNC pattern boards, traditional casting.

## Flight of Butterflies
- 29 six-foot sculptures, Chicago parks and the Magnificent Mile, April 2024 to September 2025, 18 months on view. Site says 2024 only.
- ChiLab designed and fabricated the weather-ready blank; 29 local artists each adorned one.
- Curated and managed by Cortney Lederer, CNL Projects. In the spirit of the museum's Judy Istock Butterfly Haven.
- "A clean canvas, built to last a year outdoors": welded steel base, formed aluminium wings, light enough to site anywhere, rigid enough to stand unattended.
- Acrylic inserts CNC-cut to fit the wing openings gave select designs translucent depth.
- STUDIO STAKE: ChiLab reconfigured its own space to host 29 artists working on site.
- Each modelled on a real butterfly, eastern tiger swallowtail to the state-threatened regal fritillary. CONFLICT: site says two motifs only.
- QR code at each site opened an AR experience. Second life as a travelling exhibition.
- Cortney Lederer: "They immediately responded with concepts and ideas... they quickly redesigned their studio to accommodate artists working on site. Twenty-nine artists participated and all required different logistical needs and material expertise — and ChiLab delivered."
- The model: ChiLab engineers and fabricates the object; partner artists give each one its character.

## Uber Freight (uber-spiral-stair + uber-feature-wall)
- Gensler led interiors, brought ChiLab in for two centrepieces. Halted a year by COVID, resumed on a compressed timeline.
- Feature wall: aerial view of a freight yard, gridded containers stacked at varied heights, abstracted into surface; "echoes the flow of data through Uber's logistics network." 80 cast aluminium panels patinated to oil-rubbed bronze.
- Process: parametric algorithm; 3D-printed sand molds driven directly from the digital file, no intermediary pattern; cast then hand-finished.
- "Our signature capability — bridging computation and foundry craft, straight from digital file to cast metal."
- Stair: helix of steel and reclaimed wood that appears to float; references the building's original mail chutes.
- Z-clip connection system: custom clips for minute on-site adjustment against a century-old building's inconsistencies.
- Reclaimed Chicago wood from decommissioned Chicago buildings, "grounding the stair in the city's own fabric."
- STUDIO STAKE: ChiLab's first union job and first large-scale historic-building installation.
- Ally Reza: "Challenges like these have a way of pushing us to do our very best work."
- Credits: Gensler; millwork Parenti & Raffaelli; union installation MTH Industries.
- CONFLICT: deck says steel + reclaimed wood; site says custom blue patina and infinity mirror.

## Knoll Chicago Design Center (knoll-marquee)
- Signature sculptural metalwork for Knoll's Chicago flagship, "executed at speed and at a high level of craft."
- Opened 10 June 2019, later featured in Interior Design magazine.
- Logo marquee designed by Gensler, developed by ChiLab through a sample-and-approval process; samples produced in both steel and bronze.
- Materials: silicon bronze, chrome tubing, darkened-patina bronze.
- ~150 custom components, ~300 cut and welded caps, 4-5 week fast track.
- SCOPE MISSING FROM SITE: steel handrail covers throughout, and a 24-foot solid bronze recreation of Knoll's Antenna table.
- Credits: Knoll, Benjamin Pardo, Director of Design; Gensler, Helen Hopton; CNL Art Consulting, Cortney Lederer; GC Clune.

## Jardin de Gargolas
- Part of the exhibition "New Age Grotesque" at the National Museum of Mexican Art.
- Yollocalli Arts Reach is the museum's youth initiative. With artist Tim Nicholson; teaching artist Secret of Manna.
- Fall 2023 Little Village teens design garden gargoyles in 3D software; spring 2024 cast in metal alongside ChiLab; April 2024 unveiled in the museum courtyard.
- "A virtual design becomes a permanent object — hot liquid metal poured into molds, each student unveiling a one-of-a-kind creature."
- The concept grew from a rainwater garden and a bird bath. Finished gargoyles destined for Jardin Malinalli, the museum's youth garden in Pilsen and Little Village.
- ChiLab's role framed as fabricator AND educator: "putting professional fabrication tools and craft in the hands of young artists."
- Credits: Yollocalli, Hananne Hanafi and Vanessa Sanchez; curators Cesareo Moreno and Juan Olvera.

## Saint Alfred (st-alfred)
Fills the biggest gap on the site, which had one sentence.
- Chicago streetwear boutique flagship. Deck says 2022; site says 2023.
- A custom retail fixture and display package: ChiLab translated the retail design into a buildable, durable, cohesive steel fixture system.
- "One powder-coated steel language," engineered as a single consistent system so every element reads as part of the same store.
- Elements: metal display fixtures, modified industrial shelving, custom shoe racks, hanger bars, modular centre-display system.
- Cash counter as "the transaction point," anchor of the floor: steel-clad, Caesarstone "Raw Concrete" top, millwork by Max CNC.
- ChiLab translated retail-design drawings into shop drawings and fabrication, coordinating every trade around an active construction schedule.
- Credits: Chelsea Lombardo of Interiors + Objects as design/client contact; Max CNC millwork; Ben Stagl.
- CONFLICT: site frames this as a storefront redesign in metal, glass and millwork. The deck is a fixture package in powder-coated steel and stone.

## Boolean Lamp
- MEANING, entirely absent from the site: "Inspired by the ubiquitous aluminum clip light, the Boolean Lamp seeks to find luxury in the everyday."
- The result of an iterative design process, working with skilled artisans.
- Hand-blown one at a time by Chicago glass artisans; subtle variation in colour, texture and shape makes every lamp unique while holding a consistent quality.
- Two configurations: pendant, suspended; table, on a surface.
- Glass: smoke, umber, light blue. Base: cast concrete, aluminium, acrylic.

## Unfolding Chair
- 1941 Krueger Metal Products steel folding chair: a roughly two-dollar design conceived from excess steel, built to fold flat and nest by the hundred. "One of the most under-featured yet ubiquitous objects of the twentieth century."
- "By reproducing the chair in solid bronze and removing its ability to fold and nest, the designers unfold the design — undoing the very mechanism that defined it."
- "Denied its function, the object becomes the sculptural image of the chair — writing that image into history by undoing the thing that once made it so prolific."
- "A humble stamped-steel object, reborn as a monument to itself." ANSWERS the critique/monument/joke question: a monument.
- Selected for the Chicagoland exhibit at WANTED DESIGN, New York Design Week, 2013.
